# Personas

A persona is a description of an example user created to represent a group of real users. It is used as a surrogate user during the design process to help designers focus on user needs and goals.

A persona is not necessarily a real person, but it should be realistic enough that designers can imagine how that person would interact with the system.

Personas help designers ask questions such as:

- What would this user want to do?
- Would this feature be useful to them?
- Would they understand this interface?

Details are important because they make the persona feel real and easier to relate to during design discussions.

A persona may include:

- Name
- Age
- Occupation
- Education
- Experience
- Skills
- Personal circumstances
- Goals and concerns
- Physical abilities or limitations

### Example Persona

**Betty**

- 37 years old
- Warehouse Manager for five years
- Works at the same company for twelve years
- Studied for a business diploma
- Has two children
- Does not like working late
- Limited computer training
- Slight restriction in right-hand movement after an accident
- Enthusiastic and supportive of her staff
- Feels threatened by the introduction of another new computer system

This information helps designers understand how Betty might react to new technology and what support she may need.

Personas keep the design focused on real user needs rather than designer assumptions and provide a consistent reference throughout the design process.
