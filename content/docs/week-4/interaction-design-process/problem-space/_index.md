# Understanding the Problem Space

Understanding the problem space is one of the first activities in the interaction design process. Before designing a solution, designers must understand the existing situation, the users involved, and the reasons why change is needed.

The purpose is to explore questions such as:

- What is the current user experience?
- Why is a change necessary?
- How will the proposed change improve the situation?

Problem-space exploration helps teams avoid making assumptions about users or their needs. Instead of immediately focusing on solutions, designers investigate the actual problems that users face.

Articulating the problem space is usually a collaborative activity. Team members contribute different perspectives, helping to build a more accurate understanding of the situation. This process encourages discussion, questioning, and evidence-based decision making.

A well-defined problem space:

- Clarifies the goals of the project
- Identifies user needs and challenges
- Prevents unsupported claims and incorrect assumptions
- Provides a foundation for generating effective design solutions

By thoroughly understanding the problem space, designers are more likely to create products that address real user needs rather than perceived ones.
