# Lifecycle Models

A lifecycle model describes how interaction design activities are organized and carried out throughout a project. Lifecycle models provide structure for planning, designing, prototyping, and evaluating interactive systems.

Interaction design lifecycle models are typically user-centered and emphasize continuous feedback, iteration, and improvement.

## Simple Interaction Design Lifecycle Model

A simple interaction design lifecycle model exemplifies a user-centered design approach.

The model consists of four main activities:

1. Discovering requirements
2. Designing alternatives
3. Prototyping alternative designs
4. Evaluating the designs

Evaluation can occur throughout the process, and the results often lead to revisiting earlier activities. This makes the lifecycle iterative rather than strictly sequential.

The process continues until the design satisfies user requirements and usability goals.

## Google Design Sprints

Google Design Sprints provide a structured and rapid approach to solving design problems.

The process is designed to:

- Quickly understand problems
- Generate ideas
- Create prototypes
- Test solutions with users

Design Sprints focus on obtaining user feedback early so that teams can validate ideas before investing significant development effort.

## Research in the Wild

Research in the Wild is a framework used to study technologies and user behavior in real-world settings rather than controlled laboratory environments.

The approach focuses on:

- Understanding technology use in everyday life
- Observing real user behavior
- Investigating how technologies fit into natural environments
- Collecting insights from authentic contexts

Research in the Wild helps designers understand factors that may not appear in controlled testing environments.

## Purpose of Lifecycle Models

Lifecycle models help teams:

- Organize interaction design activities
- Support iterative development
- Incorporate user feedback
- Manage design decisions
- Improve the quality of the final product

Different projects may use different lifecycle models, but all aim to support the creation of effective and user-centered interactive systems.
