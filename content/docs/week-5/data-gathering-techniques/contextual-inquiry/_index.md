# Contextual Inquiry

Contextual Inquiry is a data gathering technique that involves observing and interviewing users in their natural environment while they perform real tasks.

It originated as part of Contextual Design but is also used independently to gather requirements.

## Purpose

The goal of Contextual Inquiry is to understand how people perform activities in their everyday work or home environments.

Researchers observe users in context and discuss activities as they occur.

## Main Characteristics

- One-on-one field interviews.
- Conducted in the user's normal environment.
- Usually lasts between 1.5 and 2 hours.
- Focuses on activities relevant to the project.
- Combines observation and interviewing.

## Master–Apprentice Model

Contextual Inquiry uses a relationship similar to:

| Role | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| Master | The participant who performs the task and explains their work. |
| Apprentice | The researcher who observes, learns, and asks questions. |

The participant teaches the researcher how the work is performed.

## Four Principles of Contextual Inquiry

| Principle | Description |
|------------|-------------|
| Context | Observe users where they normally work or live while tasks are being performed. |
| Partnership | Researcher and participant work together to understand activities. |
| Interpretation | Observations are interpreted jointly by the participant and researcher. |
| Focus | Attention remains on aspects relevant to the project. |

## Cool Concepts

Some contextual inquiries are guided by "cool concepts" that help explore user experiences.

### Joy of Life Concepts

These examine how products enrich people's lives.

- Accomplishment
- Connection
- Identity
- Sensation

### Joy of Use Concepts

These examine the experience of using a product.

- Direct in Action
- Hassle Factor
- Learning Delta

## Interview Structure

A contextual inquiry interview typically consists of four stages:

1. Overview
2. Transition
3. Main Interview
4. Wrap-up

## After the Interview

Following the interview:

- An interpretation session is conducted.
- Findings are discussed and analyzed.
- Contextual design models are created or refined.
- The most relevant models are selected for further design work.

## Benefits

- Provides rich information about real work practices.
- Reveals user needs that may not emerge in traditional interviews.
- Helps understand tasks within their natural context.
- Supports accurate requirement discovery.
- Produces insights that guide design decisions.
