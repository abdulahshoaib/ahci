# Principles, Standards, and Guidelines

Principles, standards, and guidelines are different types of design rules used to improve usability. They vary in both authority (how strongly they must be followed) and generality (how widely they apply).

| Type | Authority | Generality | Purpose |
|--------|-----------|-----------|---------|
| Principles | Low | High | Provide general usability concepts |
| Guidelines | Medium | Medium | Recommend good design practices |
| Standards | High | Low | Define specific rules that must be followed |

## Principles

Principles are abstract design rules that describe general ideas about good design. They are not mandatory and do not specify exactly how a system should be implemented.

Characteristics:

- Low authority
- High generality
- Broad and conceptual
- Applicable across many systems

Examples:

- Design for recognition rather than recall
- Make systems easy to learn
- Provide clear feedback
- Support user goals

Principles help designers understand what makes a system usable rather than prescribing exact solutions.

## Standards

Standards are formal and specific design rules established by national or international organizations. They are intended to ensure consistency, compatibility, safety, and compliance across products and systems.

Characteristics:

- High authority
- Specific requirements
- Limited application
- Often mandatory

Examples:

- ISO standards
- Accessibility regulations
- Hardware interface standards

Standards are usually based on established theory and stable technologies. They change slowly and are often enforced by organizations, industries, or governments.

Examples:

| Standard | Purpose |
|-----------|---------|
| ISO 9241 | Defines usability in terms of effectiveness, efficiency, and satisfaction |
| Accessibility Laws | Ensure systems can be used by people with disabilities |

## Why Not Use Standards for Everything?

Standards cannot cover every design situation because:

- They are often too rigid
- Different systems have different requirements
- Innovation may require solutions not covered by existing standards
- New technologies evolve faster than standards

## Guidelines

Guidelines are recommendations that suggest good design practices. Unlike standards, they are flexible and can be adapted to different situations.

Characteristics:

- Medium authority
- More general than standards
- Recommended rather than mandatory
- Allow designer judgment

Examples:

- Use blue color for hyperlinks
- Place navigation in consistent locations
- Use meaningful icons and labels

Guidelines may exist at different levels:

| Type | Usage |
|--------|-------|
| Abstract Guidelines | Used during early design activities |
| Detailed Guidelines (Style Guides) | Used during interface implementation and refinement |

Understanding the reasons behind guidelines helps designers resolve conflicts when multiple guidelines suggest different solutions.

## Relationship Between Principles, Standards, and Guidelines

| Feature | Principles | Guidelines | Standards |
|----------|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| Authority | Low | Medium | High |
| Flexibility | High | Moderate | Low |
| Specificity | Low | Medium | High |
| Scope | Broad | Moderate | Narrow |
| Mandatory | No | Usually No | Often Yes |

Together, principles, guidelines, and standards provide designers with a framework for creating systems that are usable, consistent, effective, and compliant with established requirements.
