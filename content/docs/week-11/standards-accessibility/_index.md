# Standards and Accessibility

Standards are formal design rules established by national or international organizations to ensure consistency, quality, safety, and compliance across products and systems.

They provide a common set of requirements that designers and developers should follow when building interactive systems.

Standards typically have:

- High authority
- Specific requirements
- Limited application
- Formal enforcement

Unlike guidelines and principles, standards are often mandatory.

## Why Standards Exist

Standards help ensure that systems:

- Behave consistently
- Meet quality requirements
- Remain compatible with other systems
- Support usability
- Comply with legal and industry requirements

They allow large communities of designers and developers to follow common practices.

## Characteristics of Standards

- Created by national or international bodies
- Based on established theory and practice
- Change slowly over time
- Usually more common in hardware than software
- High authority and low level of detail

Because standards must work across many organizations and products, they are usually stable and carefully developed.

## Examples of Standards

### ISO Standards

The :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0} develops international standards for many industries, including usability and human-computer interaction.

One important usability standard is:

### ISO 9241

ISO 9241 defines usability as:

- Effectiveness
- Efficiency
- Satisfaction

A system is considered usable when users can successfully complete tasks, do so efficiently, and have a satisfactory experience.

## Accessibility Standards

Accessibility standards ensure that systems can be used by people with different abilities and needs.

In many cases, accessibility requirements are enforced through laws and regulations.

Failure to comply may create legal, ethical, and usability issues.

Accessibility standards help ensure that:

- Information is perceivable
- Interfaces are operable
- Content is understandable
- Systems are usable by a wide range of users

### Examples of Accessibility Support

- Keyboard navigation
- Alternative text for images
- Sufficient color contrast
- Screen reader compatibility
- Captions for multimedia
- Resizable text

Accessibility improves usability not only for people with disabilities but for all users.

## Standards vs Other Design Rules

| Type | Authority | Purpose |
|--------|-----------|---------|
| Principles | Low | General usability concepts |
| Guidelines | Medium | Recommended design practices |
| Standards | High | Formal requirements that should be followed |

## Why Not Use Standards for Everything?

Standards cannot solve every design problem because:

- They can be too rigid
- Different systems have different requirements
- New technologies evolve rapidly
- Innovation often requires flexibility

For this reason, designers typically combine standards with guidelines, principles, and design patterns when creating interactive systems.
