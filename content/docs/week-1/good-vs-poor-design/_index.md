# Good vs Poor Design

Good design helps users understand what actions are possible, what is happening now, and what will happen next. Poor design forces users to guess, remember hidden rules, or recover from avoidable mistakes.

## Good Design

- Makes important actions visible.
- Gives timely and meaningful feedback.
- Uses familiar patterns consistently.
- Prevents errors where possible.
- Supports recovery when errors happen.
- Fits the user's real task and context.

## Poor Design

- Hides available actions.
- Uses unclear labels or misleading icons.
- Gives little or no feedback.
- Places similar actions too close together.
- Uses inconsistent layouts or terminology.
- Blames users for mistakes caused by the interface.

## HCI Perspective

Design quality is judged by how well the system supports human activity, not by appearance alone.
