# Applied Human Computer Interaction {anchor=false}

Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) studies how people design, use, and evaluate interactive systems. It connects computing with human behavior, cognition, design, ergonomics, and social context.

## What Is HCI?

HCI is about making technology useful, usable, accessible, and meaningful for people. It asks how users understand systems, how interfaces support or block user goals, and how design decisions affect real interaction.

Good HCI does not only make screens look better. It improves the relationship between users and systems by reducing confusion, preventing errors, supporting learning, and giving users clear control.

## Why It Matters

- People have limited attention, memory, and time.
- Poor design causes mistakes, frustration, and wasted effort.
- Interactive systems must fit real users, tasks, and contexts.
- Evaluation helps teams find problems before they become expensive.
- Accessibility and inclusion make systems usable by more people.

## Core Themes

- Interaction design and user experience.
- Human cognition, perception, memory, and learning.
- User-centered design and design process.
- Usability evaluation and prototyping.
- Interaction styles, devices, and interface paradigms.
- Social, ethical, and contextual effects of technology.
